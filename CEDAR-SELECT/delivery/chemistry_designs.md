## Evidence and hypothesis

The supplied eight-member analogue packet already argues against choosing the most potent fluorinated ether. Example23 has 15 nM CDK2 and 2.43 µM CDK1 (162-fold); example39 has 7 nM and 1.20 µM (~171-fold); example41 has 5 nM and 0.73 µM (146-fold); example56 has 10 nM and 0.80 µM (80-fold). Thus fluorination changes potency on both enzymes, with no convincing monotonic discrimination improvement. Example69/70 has identical CDK2 values and nearly identical CDK1 values; the supplied stereopair does not establish a stereochemical selectivity pocket. Example77's free pyrazolol loses CDK2 potency and discrimination, so deleting the ether is a poor progression hypothesis. These are source values without independent error estimates, not new measurements.

A more useful test comes from the wider supplied public context. Ring expansion from N-(1R,3R)-hydroxycyclohexyl to the corresponding cycloheptyl with OMe changes CDK2 from 20 to 114 nM while CDK1 goes from 2.24 µM to a lower bound of 10 µM. The resulting selectivity bound is only ≥87.7-fold, so **improved discrimination has not been demonstrated**: the CDK1 ceiling could conceal either a benefit or loss relative to 112-fold. With the pyrazole methyl substituent the same ring change gives 18→41 nM CDK2 and 1.21→4.49 µM CDK1 (~67→110-fold). This weak interaction evidence justifies a small factorial experiment, not a general ring-expansion rule. Exact supplied pointers (zero-based) are `public_assay_context.json:/train/18,/train/19` for ring7 methyl (HC-2d0718c2679a9918f84b70f2), `/train/20` and `/bounds_context_only/4` for ring7 methoxy (HC-862148e6286362a6b2359a14); the methyl ring6 parent is `/train/0,/train/1`. These precedents do not warrant additive potency or selectivity predictions.

A ratio improvement under one condition alone is insufficient. The 0.5-log threshold is a decision margin, not a known assay error. Fit paired log IC50s and confidence intervals; when CDK1 is censored, report a lower bound and avoid ranking overlapping bounds. Extend the CDK1 range only if solubility/assay interference permit. If no design clears the margin, stop this edit rather than reinterpret subthreshold potency as selectivity.

## Public route precedent and proposed execution

[US20240360137A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20240360137A1/en), Intermediate1/2 and examples2,12,23,39, supplies modular chlorospiro-core/aminopyrazole chemistry. Example39 installs fluoroethoxy by Cs2CO3-mediated O-alkylation of N-SEM nitropyrazolol with fluoroethyl iodide (83.61% reported); nitro reduction precedes coupling. Example2 couples the protected chlorocore and aminopyrazole with BrettPhos-Pd/NaOtBu, then acid removes protecting groups. Intermediate2 substitutes a cycloheptyl building block. Example12's coupling paragraph repeats the product name as starting substrate; use the explicit chlorocore in example2 and original scheme for planning, not that erroneous sentence. Reported coupling/deprotection yields are precedents, not forecasts.

Our route proposal for D1/D2 is to prepare and analytically qualify the (1R,3R) cycloheptyl chlorospiro intermediate once, split it, and couple to separately qualified ethoxy and fluoroethoxy protected aminopyrazoles. After deprotection, chromatographic purification and palladium removal, target 10–20 mg per compound for biochemical and early cell studies. Retain intermediates for troubleshooting. This is a multistep route from commodity precursors, not a two-step synthesis unless both advanced partners are actually in hand. Chiral identity is a critical path: source-assigned stereochemistry must be linked to a trusted precursor or orthogonal assignment, not inferred from a retention-time order. Specify identity by NMR/HRMS and purity ≥95% with quantitative concentration check; aim ≥98% stereoisomer purity and document absolute-configuration basis. LCMS alone cannot assign ether regiochemistry or configuration.

D3/D4 proposal: prepare both enantiomers of 1-fluoropropan-2-ol (or a racemate with preparative chiral separation), install their ether on protected nitropyrazolol using an optimized substitution/Mitsunobu route, then reduction, common ring6-core coupling and deprotection. Secondary substitution can eliminate or produce incorrect O/N alkylation; neither yield nor stereoretention is presumed. Track inversion where relevant and assign final configuration independently. A two-day microscale feasibility test should precede committing scarce advanced core. These routes are chemically plausible proposals, not publicly verified procedures for the final designs.

## Alternatives beyond this packet

The primary [INX-315 study](https://incyclixbio.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Cancer-Discovery-2024-INX-315.pdf) provides a more developed alternative chemical benchmark in CCNE1-amplified and CDK4/6-inhibitor-resistant models. It warrants a qualified reference compound and matched assays if available, not transferring its activity to our designs or directly pooling its IC50s. It supports testing dependency and durable cell-cycle effects rather than treating a biochemical ratio as sufficient. No supplier availability or rights to use a patented series were established here.

The primary [anthranilic-acid allosteric study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10239507/) reports nanomolar binding and structural confirmation, but strong negative cooperativity with cyclin: high-affinity compound4 poorly inhibits preassembled CDK/cyclin complexes. Its selectivity is therefore an interesting mechanism alternative, not a drop-in substitute for a CDK2/E1 inhibitor. The programme would need to establish monomer occupancy, order-of-addition dependence and inhibition under relevant cyclin abundance before funding an oncology switch. Its contraceptive/explant evidence does not establish a cancer-cell window. Do not use ATP-site docking to rank this series against spiro-lactams. On current evidence, reserve this as a mechanism experiment rather than the primary 30-day synthesis campaign.
